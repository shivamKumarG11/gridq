1 Problem

Choose plants to minimise cost and cover demand + reserve.

C(x) = Σᵢ cᵢ xᵢ + λ (Σᵢ pᵢ xᵢ − 1)²

xᵢ ∈ {0,1}: plant i off/on · cᵢ: no-load cost + minimum-output premium · pᵢ = Pmaxᵢ / target · λ = 6 · target = demand + reserve + 60 MW. The QUBO is only a guide: every sampled plan is re-scored with the true cost.

Bars: each plant's linear weight after expanding the penalty (amber = negative, pulls the plant ON; cyan = positive).

The one claim

          
Can QAOA find good power-plant schedules under demand swings, and how does it compare with classical search at the same budget?

          
A useful answer either way: if QAOA loses, we show by how much, where, and why.

          
Fair test

          
QAOA, simulated annealing and random search get the same budget of true-cost evaluations per hour, and QAOA's training is charged to it. Every candidate is scored by the same exact dispatch and feasibility rules, and the same day-stitcher links the hours for all of them. An exact MILP solves the same, fully linked 24-hour problem, so the gap we report is against the real optimum.

          
Hours are linked

          
Start-up costs (hot or cold) and minimum up/down times connect the hours. The stitcher (beam search) enforces them: a proposal that breaks a rule is repaired by keeping or adding the cheapest allowed plants, and the number of repairs is reported.

          
Impact you can see

          
Shift demand or add solar and run the day again: the world shows which plants start, what it costs, and where QAOA and the optimum disagree.

        

        

          
How a day is solved

          
    
            
  1. QUBO per hour: 10 binary decisions, 1 qubit per plant.
    2. 
            
  2. QAOA: angles trained once on the peak hour with the exact statevector, then reused for every hour (parameter transfer).
    3. 
            
  3. Sample: the Qiskit circuit runs on the backend you chose in Qollab's dialog. IonQ backends take one job, so the two peak hours are packed side by side into one 20-qubit circuit and the other hours use the ideal statevector.
    4. 
            
  4. Score: every distinct sampled plan gets its true cost by economic dispatch (equal incremental cost).
    5. 
            
  5. Stitch: the best 6 plans per hour go to the shared beam-search stitcher.
    6. 
            
  6. Compare: against simulated annealing, random search, the priority-list heuristic and the exact MILP (HiGHS).

What the demo establishes

              
  • How close QAOA gets to the true optimum on the standard 10-plant benchmark
  • How it compares with classical search at equal budget
  • How much IonQ-style noise changes the answer

            

            

              
What it does not claim

              
  • No quantum advantage: at 10 plants a laptop finds the exact optimum instantly
  • Not ready to operate a real grid (no network flows, ramp limits or outages)
  • The solar day is stylised, not measured data
